# Cosmos — The Evolution of Everything

A real-time, explorable 3D model of the universe from one second after the Big Bang, through the present (13.8 billion years), and on into a ΛCDM projection of the future. Fly through it, orbit a galaxy, and drag cosmic time backward and forward.

Built with Three.js, TypeScript and Vite. No assets, no data files: the whole universe is generated from a seed in the browser.

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| Look | drag |
| Fly | `W A S D` / arrows, `Q` `E` down / up, scroll or pinch, `Shift` to boost (touch: hold **Go**) |
| Orbit | click a galaxy, scroll to zoom, click empty space or `Esc` to leave |
| Time | `Space` play / pause, `,` `.` step, drag the timeline, tap a marker to jump to an epoch, **Now** returns to the present |

URL options: `?q=high|mid|low` (quality), `?seed=1234`, `?u=0.86` (start position on the timeline), `?paused`.

## What is physically modelled

**Expansion** (`src/cosmology`). Flat ΛCDM, Friedmann equation `H² = H0² (Ωr a⁻⁴ + Ωm a⁻³ + ΩΛ)` with Planck 2018 parameters: H0 = 67.66 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3111, Ωr = 9.14×10⁻⁵ (photons + 3.046 neutrino species), ΩΛ = 1 − Ωm − Ωr, T_CMB = 2.7255 K. Cosmic time `t(a) = ∫ da / (a H)` is integrated numerically and inverted, so the scale factor, redshift, temperature, deceleration parameter and linear growth factor are pure functions of time. The model gives an age of 13.79 Gyr, z ≈ 1076 at t = 380,000 yr, and the switch from decelerating to accelerating expansion at z ≈ 0.64.

**Structure** (`src/sim`). A Gaussian random density field with a CDM-like spectrum is generated by FFT, and matter moves in comoving coordinates by the Zel'dovich approximation, `x(q, t) = q + D(t) ψ(q)`, with `D` the ΛCDM growth factor. This produces sheets, filaments, knots and voids, and structure growth freezes in the future as dark energy takes over. The densest knots are identified as gravitationally bound groups and clusters.

**Expansion vs. bound structures.** On-screen position is `σ · (x − x_observer) + offset`. The comoving part is multiplied by the view scale σ; the `offset` of matter inside a bound structure is a fixed physical vector that is not. Galaxies and clusters therefore move apart while keeping their own size.

**No centre, no edge.** The volume is periodic and drawn relative to the observer, who is always a comoving observer. Wherever you fly, everything recedes from you in proportion to its distance, and there is no boundary to reach.

**Time.** The scene is a function of cosmic time only (`visualState(t)` plus static GPU buffers). Scrubbing backward gives exactly the states seen going forward.

## What is illustrative — read this before quoting it

- **Procedural, not observed.** No survey or simulation data is used. The cosmic web here is statistically plausible, not a map of the real sky.
- **View scale is compressed.** The true scale factor grows by ten orders of magnitude over the timeline. On-screen separations follow `σ(a)`, a monotonic compression of it (logarithmic early, ∝ √a later; see `viewScale` in `src/sim/kinematics.ts`). The read-out always reports the true value.
- **Sizes and contrasts are exaggerated.** Galaxies are drawn roughly ten times too large relative to their separations; early density ripples and CMB anisotropies (really ~1 part in 100,000) are amplified to be visible.
- **Colours are a guide.** The early plasma cools from blue-white through gold to orange; the true spectrum at those temperatures lies far outside visible light for most of that time.
- **Early-universe simplifications.** Temperature uses `T = T0 / a` throughout (ignoring the change in particle species around electron–positron annihilation), recombination is a smooth switch between ~4100 K and ~2650 K, and the growth-factor formula is exact only for matter + Λ.
- **Nothing before one second.** The Big Bang itself, inflation and the first second are not shown.
- **The future is a projection.** Everything right of “Now” assumes a cosmological constant and is marked as such.

## Development

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # local dev server
npm test           # cosmology, kinematics and determinism checks (vitest)
npm run build      # type-check + production build to dist/
```

Layout: `cosmology/` (expansion history, timeline) · `sim/` (density field, structure, kinematics, visual state) · `render/` (GLSL, layers) · `camera/` · `ui/` · `main.ts` (orchestration).

Deployed on Vercel as a static Vite build.
